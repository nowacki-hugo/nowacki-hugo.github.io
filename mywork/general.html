HOW DID WE GET HERE ?

This fundamental interogation is the overarching motor of my whole research. By means of observations, I aim at understanding how planets prone to host life form within their protoplanetary disks. I also contribute to the making of cutting edge instrumentation, enabling to resolve insanely small regions.

Broad presentation that sends to the individual pages.

  • Exoplanet characterisation

Not only is it interesting to understand the diversity of exoplanets, but their formation is still very speculative as it is a process thought to happen pretty fast, in regions where it is difficult to observe it. Communication to the grand public

It is a part of the responsibility of any scientist to make sure that the concepts they work on are transmited to the grand public, as the science we produce is a common good. To do my part, I regularly participate in public observations of the night sky. Stargazing with strangers has always been a great way for me to connect to people and to make them understand the importance of research in general, not just in astrophysics. I also realized two podcasts. One of them was hosted by Université Grenoble Alpes (UGA), as an episode of the "Allodocs" series. Each episode of this series aims at answering a naive question directly adressed by the science performed by the speaker, who is a Ph.D. candidate of UGA. The sixth episode of season 3 explains the difference between a star and a planet (in French) : I realized another podcast describing the work I performed during my Ph.D. more in details. This time, the episode was part of a short series aiming at describing the work of Ph.D. candidates at IPAG : "Podcastronomie" (still in French).
