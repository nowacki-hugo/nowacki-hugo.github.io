HOW DID WE GET HERE ?

This fundamental interogation is the overarching motor of my whole research. By means of observations, I aim at understanding how planets prone to host life form within their protoplanetary disks. I also contribute to the making of cutting edge instrumentation, enabling to resolve insanely small regions.

Broad presentation that sends to the individual pages.

  • Exoplanet characterisation

Not only is it interesting to understand the diversity of exoplanets, but their formation is still very speculative as it is a process thought to happen pretty fast, in regions where it is difficult to observe it. Namely, hidden within the thickness of protoplanetary disks. To understand the mechanisms responsible for the exoplanets we know, I also study their parent objects :

  • Young Stellar Objects

The majority of my research relies on optical long-baseline interferometry. I also have an instrumental implication in this technique. During my Ph.D., I was responsible for the characterization, optimization, and validation of one key element of the Very Large Telescope Interferometer (VLTI) of the European Southern Observatory (ESO) on top of the Cerro Paranal, in the Atacama desert of Chile.

  • GRAVITY+ Adaptive Optics (GPAO) deformable mirrors

The VLTI is the most sensitive interferometer in the world. But I am also involved in instrumental developments of the observatory with the highest resolution : the Center for High Angular Resolution Astronomy (CHARA).

  • SPICA-FT fringe tracker

Outreach

It is part of the responsibility of every scientist to make sure that the concepts they work on are transmited to the grand public, as the science we produce is a common good. To do my part, I regularly participate in public observations of the night sky. Stargazing with strangers has always been a great way for me to connect with people and to make them understand the importance of research in general, not just in astrophysics. I also realized two podcasts. One of them was hosted by Université Grenoble Alpes (UGA), as an episode of the "Allodocs" series. Each episode of this series aims at answering a naive question directly adressed by the science performed by the speaker, who is a Ph.D. candidate of UGA. The sixth episode of season 3 explains the difference between a star and a planet (in French) : I realized another podcast describing the work I performed during my Ph.D. more in details. This time, the episode was part of a short series aiming at describing the work of Ph.D. candidates at IPAG : "Podcastronomie" (still in French).
