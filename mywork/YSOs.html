Motivations

Young Stellar Objects have been an increasing topic of interest over the last decade, particularly since the pioneering observation of the protoplanetary disk of HL Tau system by the ALMA array.

In the context of planet formation, understanding the structure, chemistry and dynamical evolution of these disks is key to unveil the mechanisms responsible for planet formation.

As a matter of fact, they serve both as the birthplace and reservoir of material for planets. The exact timescale and location of planet formation are still a hot topic among the comunity however, and extensive observation campaigns have been carried out with very different techinques in order to unveil the mysteries of planet formation.

Young Stellar objects are located in dense ster forming regions and therefore are faint. The closest star forming regions to Earth being beyond 100 parsec, they are also very small.

A particularly fruitful approach in observing these objects has lied in the synergies built by large consortia over the years, combining high angular resolution techniques and high spectral resolution techniques.

My work on YSOs is situated at a crossroad between two avenues of YSOs observations. The first one : spectropolarimetry, the second : optical interferometry.

It has been the originality of my Ph.D. work to combine these two methods in order to paint the most precise picture of the region located with the orbit of the Earth.

The S CrA N system
